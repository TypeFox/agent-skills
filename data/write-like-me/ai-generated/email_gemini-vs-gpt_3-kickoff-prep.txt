Perfect, thanks. The scope and artifacts you’ve prepared sound exactly right for the PoC.

On the validation messages: I would **not rewrite them specifically for the AI**. The best diagnostics are clear, precise, and actionable for both humans and machines.

For example, instead of:

> Invalid sensor configuration.

we’d prefer something along the lines of:

> Nexus-Core supports at most two high-frequency OptiTrack sensors; this configuration contains three.

Even better, we can attach structured information to the diagnostic internally: a stable rule identifier, the affected components, the actual and allowed values, and potentially a suggested correction. The editor can turn that into a concise message for the user, while the AI gets enough context to understand exactly what failed and how it might repair the configuration.

That separation is useful because we don’t want the validate-and-repair loop to depend on interpreting cleverly worded prose. The validator should remain deterministic and domain-specific; the LLM can then take those facts and explain them conversationally when needed.

For the PoC, I suggest we establish this pattern for the power-budgeting and cardinality rules and see how well it supports both of your target workflows. That should also give us a good template for migrating additional constraints later.
